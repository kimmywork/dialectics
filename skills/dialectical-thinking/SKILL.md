# Dialectical Thinking: An Operating Manual for Complex Dilemmas and Tensions

> Methodology distilled from Kimmy's *Beyond Either/Or: A Popular Handbook of Dialectical Thinking* (《走出二选一》, dialectics.kimleo.net). Companion to the `critical-thinking` skill.

## 0. Always triage first: pick the right tool before solving

For any conflict, first determine which domain it belongs to:

| Domain | Typical scenarios | Primary tool | Forbidden move |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fact verification / causal attribution / contract compliance | Auditing, acceptance testing, accident attribution, scientific checks, breach determination | Formal logic + `critical-thinking` + empirical verification | Never use "everything has two sides" or "blame both sides" to cover up factual errors or breach of duty |
| Multi-goal allocation under fixed resources | Budget splits, scheduling, savings vs. spending | §6 main contradiction + marginal-return calculation | Never spread effort thin or demand "X and Y and Z all at once" — a single P0 must be ranked |
| Mutually dependent, long-lived tensions | Exploit vs. explore, centralize vs. empower, parenting (control vs. autonomy), closeness vs. independence | This skill, §1–§8 in full | Never pick one pole black-and-white or try to eliminate one pole once and for all |

Ticking-clock crises (fire escape, acute rescue, violence in progress, financial fraud): **dialectical analysis is forbidden** — act first to stop the bleeding / call for help / isolate, and review later (see §10 failure boundaries).

## 1. Diagnosis: four tension types + the three-question test

**Logical bedrock (Han Feizi's spear-and-shield)**: two mutually exclusive factual assertions about the same object, at the same time, on the same concrete dimension cannot both be true — at least one is a lie, a miscalculation, or conceptual confusion. Rule out factual falsehoods before discussing dialectical tension; logical contradictions (cooked books, inconsistent words and deeds) can never be whitewashed as "dialectical unity."

| Tension type | Core structure | Correct response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dissolvable pseudo-contradiction | Ambiguous wording, mixed levels, information mismatch ("demo release" ≠ "full production release") | Clarify definitions, split dimensions — the conflict vanishes on the spot |
| Trade-off | Independent goals competing for the same resource pool, no intrinsic interdependence (fish vs. bear's paw) | Compute value, rank explicitly; choosing ends the matter |
| Dilemma | Two mutually exclusive options each carrying a heavy cost; one must be chosen | Weigh the lesser evil, cut decisively, and honestly accept the cost |
| Paradox | Superficially opposed, but each is the other's condition of existence; pursuing one pole alone invites backlash (tension vs. rest; explore vs. exploit) | Long-term dynamic management — time/space splitting + structural layering + rhythm management |

**Three-question test (3 minutes to classify)**:
1. **Dissolution test**: After breaking the contested key word into more concrete scenarios or levels, does the conflict still exist? No → pseudo-contradiction.
2. **Interdependence test**: Push A to 100% and zero out B — can A itself survive long-term? Yes (choosing bear's paw, the fish never returns) → trade-off/dilemma; No (cutting R&D to save costs, two years later even the old products stop selling) → paradox.
3. **Return-tide test**: After you grit your teeth and choose, does the abandoned pole come back in a new form months later (strict discipline → nobody dares innovate → you now hold meetings begging for experimentation)? Yes → paradox.

**Watch for the inflation fallacy**: ordinary trade-offs caused by insufficient budget, greed, or procrastination are not profound paradoxes. Use dissolution and calculation to clear 80% of problems; save dialectical energy for the 20% that are genuinely interdependent.

## 2. Boundary check: has the question itself overreached?

Dead-loop signal: both sides argue rigorously and self-consistently, forever, with no resolution → the problem is not the answers, it is the question.

- **Universal overreach words**: "essentially," "fundamentally," "really," "always," "unconditionally," "100%." Questions containing them exceed the bounds of empirical testability ("What do post-95s *essentially* care about?" → rewrite as "What were the top three concrete reasons post-95 engineers left our company in the past six months?").
- **Mixed dimensions**: the two sides are measuring the same thing with rulers from different dimensions (group statistical probability vs. individual extreme case; long-term strategy vs. short-term cash flow) → separate the dimensions before conversing.
- **Presupposition smuggling**: the question's syntax welds in a hidden premise ("When did you stop beating your wife?"). Refuse to answer within the question's frame; dismantle the hidden premise first.
- **Convert unanswerable ultimatum questions into action protocols**: "Do you really love me / can you absolutely guarantee it?" → don't mount a defense; convert into a concrete-scenario action protocol ("If your fever exceeds 38.5°C, I take leave and come home immediately").
- **The reverse error is equally forbidden**: clearly checkable factual questions (a budget overrun of 3 million) may not be waved off as "that's a philosophical question with no clear answer."

## 3. Exposure and clarification: stress tests and unblinding

**Dissecting others**:
- **Self-reference test (Mozi's canon)**: for any claim containing "all / every / absolutely / never," apply the sentence to the speaker himself. "All viewpoints are just propaganda" — is that sentence itself an objective fact or propaganda? Either way it self-destructs.
- **Limit-extrapolation test (Zeno's reductio)**: assume the rule/claim holds, push its parameters to the extreme, and see where the system breaks. Mandatory for institutional design (bonuses 100% tied to five-star ratings → veterans dodge hard calls, newcomers eat the bad reviews, coupons get showered on angry customers). Reductio exists to correct beliefs, not to win quarrels.

**Unblinding yourself (Xunzi's "Jiebi" / "Release from Blindness")**:
- Ten blinders: desire, aversion, first impressions, final outcomes, distant, near, broad, shallow, past, present — each can obscure the whole. The truth about high-level disputes: everyone holds a genuine fragment; the tragedy is mistaking a fragment for the whole mirror.
- **Emptiness, unity, stillness (虚一而静)**: suspend prior assumptions (emptiness) → lay positive and negative evidence side by side on the table (unity / "present all things and weigh them") → switch off emotional defenses (stillness).
- **Steelmanning**: actively rebuild the opponent's position with its strongest premises and evidence, then examine what patches your own position needs.
- When hearing a jarring objection, first ask: "Is he standing exactly in the corner I cannot see?"

## 4. Reframing: from either/or to the third option

**Three-step reframing**:
1. Write down A₊ (core benefit of choosing A) and A₋ (worst cost); likewise B₊ and B₋.
2. Ask: what concrete obstacle stands between "A₊ and B₊ both holding" (Veraksa's "change of alternative" — rewrite "go or don't go" into "how do we go without catching cold")?
3. Rewrite the question: "What new tool, new rule, or new time point would let me capture A₊ and B₊ while blocking A₋ and B₋?"

**Key techniques**:
- **Find the mediator M**: something carrying the opposite attributes both sides need — a raincoat for "playing in rain" and "staying dry"; escrow for "trust" and "risk."
- **Constancy and expediency (Mencius — "reaching a hand to a drowning sister-in-law")**: attach an emergency-exemption valve with explicit trigger conditions to every rigid principle. Three anti-abuse conditions: ① purpose alignment (serves the institution's parent goal, not personal convenience); ② publicized trigger conditions; ③ post-hoc written record and review. Expediency everywhere makes rules putty; none at all makes you a cold doctrinaire.
- **Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) communication switch**: replace "but" with "and / at the same time" — two opposite psychological facts can both be true in the same room. "You worked extremely hard, AND the conversion rate did miss target — let's look at how to turn that effort into results."
- **Cost discipline**: any claim of "having both" must inject new resources, new processes, or remove old actions. "X and Y and Z" with no supporting mechanism is enforced-both-ways pseudo-dialectics (see §9).

## 5. Splitting and placing: timely mean and three-dimensional splitting

The optimum is not the fifty-fifty midpoint but a moving target across contexts ("going too far is as bad as falling short"; the junzi's "timely mean"). Three splitting strategies:

- **Context switching**: different modes in different settings. Split a brainstorm into two halves — first 30 minutes pure divergence (pouring cold water forbidden), next 30 minutes pure convergence and critique.
- **Time switching**: put mutually exclusive goals into alternating time windows (tension and release). Deep work with do-not-disturb mornings, batched messages at noon; a "pulse contract" — after a crunch sprint, paid recovery leave is locked into the schedule.
- **Level switching**: apply asymmetric standards at different levels. Strict with yourself (internally controllable, closed-loop, zero friction) / lenient with others (externally controllable — harshness only breeds reactance); headquarters locks the brand bottom line and financial red lines, frontline tactics fully empowered.

**Pitfall**: the timely mean is not unprincipled opportunism; every split must state its boundary conditions per dimension.

## 6. Priority: seize the main contradiction (the single bottleneck)

**Four-step procedure**:
1. **List**: write down all coexisting problems (≤ 8 items).
2. **Draw dependency arrows**: if X directly worsens Y, draw X→Y.
3. **"One move unblocks the board" test**: if you could fully solve only one thing, which one would automatically loosen the other three or four? → that source node is the true bottleneck (Theory of Constraints: efficiency gains on non-bottlenecks contribute exactly zero to system output; station C at 30 units/hour caps the whole line).
4. **Dynamic-shift review**: once a bottleneck is broken, immediately find the next one — yesterday's ox's nose may have moved today (validation phase: PMF; scaling phase: repeatable acquisition; maturity phase: organizational governance).

**Anti-patterns**: pepper-spraying (tactical busyness masking strategic laziness); the streetlight effect (tackling easy secondary problems while avoiding the hardest true bottleneck).

**Discipline**: pour 70% of incremental resources into the single bottleneck; hold the rest at passing grade with 30%. "A, B, and C are all top priority" = nothing is a priority.

## 7. The time dimension: transformation conditions, thresholds, and sublation

- **Transformation conditions**: when expecting "bad turns to good / advantages persist," you must spell out the concrete mechanism that enables the turn and the conditions that trigger backlash. A transformation prophecy without a mechanism is fortune-telling.
- **Quantitative-to-qualitative thresholds**: when consoling yourself that "we're still in the accumulation phase," name the observable leading indicator that keeps improving; if you can't, it's a dead end. Reverse direction: prevent "deviation becoming normalized" chronic slides (a thousand-foot dike collapses from an ant hole).
- **Sublation inventory (discard, keep, elevate)**: when leaving an old mode, which rigid shell gets cut (discard), which underlying core competence is retained (keep), and what new carrier is it grafted onto (elevate)? Prevent pouring out the baby with the bathwater — and also prevent fake upgrades that just circle in place.

## 8. Self-critique: pre-mortem and falsifiability

- **Pre-mortem**: after finalizing a plan, assume "one year from now it has failed utterly," and have everyone silently write three specific causes of death. This turns "finding flaws" from a social liability into a required move.
- **Red team / devil's advocate**: ask the most exacting person to tear the plan to shreds on purpose.
- **Popper test**: for every strong judgment, write out its falsification condition — by which date, upon which concrete metric, will I publicly admit error and stop the loss? A judgment with no falsification condition is obsession, not reason.
- **Healthy self-critique vs. rumination**: critique targets specific methods/assumptions/processes and exports a next corrective action ("next time scenario X appears, I do B instead of A"); self-attack targets personal worth ("I'm always like this," "my life is over") → brake immediately, rewrite as "which step did I miss in the third link of this matter."

## 9. Anti-fraud: nine pseudo-dialectics quick reference

Whenever "look at it dialectically" is used to dodge responsibility, stall, or blur facts, counter with the test question:

| Pseudo-dialectic | Typical talk | Core test question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Muddy compromise | "Meet in the middle," "split the difference" | Is the middle option mechanically superior to both ends, or a blend of both ends' flaws? |
| Premature synthesis | "Actually you're saying the same thing" | Before declaring unity, can I restate each side's strongest argument in terms they'd endorse? |
| Relativist dissolution | "Everything has two sides" | Is the evidential weight 50-50 or 99-to-1? What did saying this actually advance? |
| Sophist appropriation | "Depends how you define X" | Is this the agreed-upon meaning, or a definition rewritten after the fact to dodge blame? |
| Enforced both-ways | "Cut costs AND raise quality AND go faster" | Are the demanders and the cost-bearers the same people? Were new resources injected? |
| Stalling dialectics | "Hold the tension, let's observe" | What is the observation deadline? What specific signal forces action? |
| Logical contradiction disguised as tension | "Dynamically both compliant and pending optimization" | Can both factual judgments be true at the same time on the same metric? |
| Mechanical thesis-antithesis-synthesis | "Achievements noted, shortcomings minor, future bright" | Delete the first and last paragraphs — is any information lost? Did the "critique" hit the core problem? |
| Unfalsifiable prophecy | "Still in the accumulation phase," "fortune follows misfortune," "tuition paid is wealth gained" | By which date, at which metric, will you admit this path is dead? |

**Four decompilation moves for bureaucratic rhetoric**: posture verbs ("highly valued," "solidly advanced") → demand physical deliverables and acceptance dates; subject-evaporation sentences ("related work is proceeding in an orderly manner") → restore the five elements [who] [when] [why] [which metric deviated by how much] [who fixes it by when]; fuzzy quantifiers ("broadly under control") → demand the warning-line percentage and emergency threshold; parallel list-verbs ("X and Y and Z") → demand a ranked 1-2-3 priority order with resource balancing.

**Usage boundary**: don't dissect everyday social pleasantries. Deploy these nine only when ① someone uses rhetoric to shift unfair costs onto you or your team, or ② you are making a major decision and must not anesthetize yourself with pretty formulas.

## 10. Three failure boundaries (stop using dialectical thinking immediately when these hold)

1. **Ticking-clock crises and zero-tolerance ethics**: fire escape, acute rescue, domestic violence, crime, financial fraud → act first to stop the bleeding / call the police / isolate; never "dialectically compromise" with evil that crosses the line.
2. **Malicious manipulation under extreme power asymmetry with no exit** (gaslighting, toxic organizations) → stop unilateral introspection and reframing (otherwise dialectics becomes an opiate that excuses the abuser); shift to hardening your evidence chain, cultivating your best alternative (BATNA), and exiting decisively.
3. **Severe lack of basic facts and domain expertise** → dialectical thinking is an operating system that processes facts; it produces none itself. First master the basic data, industry base rates, and physical constraints via `critical-thinking` and professional learning, then start the dialectical toolkit.

## 11. Seven-step process for major decisions (career change, relocation, home purchase, strategy)

1. **Four-type diagnosis and overreach check** (§1, §2): strip away the pseudo-question "which path is the *perfectly* correct one"; classify the current dilemma.
2. **Unblinding interrogation** (§3): Do I want to leave because I'm escaping a specific pain point, or because I've truly seen the underlying logic of the new lane?
3. **Bottleneck lock** (§6): Is what's blocking me the platform's ceiling, or my own capability gap? (If the latter, I'll hit the same wall three months after switching companies.)
4. **Change of alternative + time splitting** (§4, §5): rewrite "resign today vs. stagnate forever" into "how do I turn the next 12 months of staying into a springboard for a high-probability transition."
5. **Sublation inventory** (§7): which assets zero out if I leave this platform (discard), which core chips travel with me (keep & elevate)?
6. **Two-sided pre-mortem** (§8): assume "the jump fails" and "staying deteriorates"; write three causes of death and probe indicators for each.
7. **Lock the tentative plan and triggers**: set an explicit decision deadline and key verification metrics. Use real-options thinking: pay a small option premium (pilot, part-time consulting, internal transfer) and decide whether to exercise after the cards are shown — never bet everything at once.

**Baseline model**: in major life decisions, linear pros-and-cons ledgers always fail — core values (health, family, cash flow) are incommensurable. First lock the hard floors under each dimension: if any one floor is breached, total utility goes to zero no matter how much the others multiply.

## 12. Panoramic scan for open complex problems: SPiRiT

When facing an unfamiliar complex topic (taking over a new department, analyzing an industry, a family crisis) with no idea where to start:

- **S — Structure**: What subsystems compose it? Which parent system does it belong to? Which two forces pull in opposite directions inside it? How does it present differently at the principles level vs. the operations level?
- **P — Process**: How did the situation evolve step by step into today? Key turning points? Which tiny accumulations (latent heat or ant holes) are building under the waterline? How long is the delay before today's action gets its rebound signal?
- **R — Relationship**: What is the antonym reference axis (each side's worst nightmare) behind the contested key word? Who causes whom? Which node jams the whole board? Who holds the irreplaceability, whose walk-away alternative is harder?
- **T — Transformation**: What concrete obstacle made us think it's either/or? How do discard/keep/elevate split? What are the three most likely causes of death if this plan fails a year from now, and what are the stop-loss metrics?

## Output conventions

- Respond in the user's language; keep established English terms when they are clearer.
- When analyzing a complex dilemma: first state the tension classification and domain triage, then proceed structurally through "diagnose → reframe → split → prioritize → pre-mortem," and end with an executable plan.
- Every plan must carry three attachments: a resource-balancing account (who bears which cost), verification thresholds (leading indicators and warning lines), and a decision deadline with triggers.
- Always rule out factual problems before riding tensions; never use dialectical talk to cover factual falsehoods.
- Proactively declare failure boundaries: when crises, malicious manipulation, or expertise gaps are involved, say so honestly and switch tools.
