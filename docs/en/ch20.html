Part VII

★ Chapter 20 Public Discourse: Staying Clear-Headed in an Age of Polarization

If families and workplaces still carry tangible material ties that pull people back toward reason, the moment we unlock our smartphones and step into the public square of social media feeds, short-video comment sections, and group chats, we enter a jungle engineered for maximum Polarization.

Here, any multidimensional public event is flattened within three hours into two warring binary camps: Camp A brands anyone in Camp B morally bankrupt, while Camp B accuses anyone in Camp A of being a paid shill. If anyone dares stand between them and say, “Hold on—this issue involves two distinct structural layers and boundary conditions,” both camps immediately turn their fire on the speaker, jeering them as a “fence-sitter,” “enlightened centrist,” or “apologist.”

Worse still, even complete grammatical sentences are vanishing from online arguments, replaced by a barrage of blood-pressure-spiking single-character combat slang—known across the Chinese internet as the “Ten-Character Online Combat Mantra”: 典 (Diǎn), 孝 (Xiào), 急 (Jí), 蚌 (Bèng), 乐 (Lè), 赢 (Yíng), 润 (Rùn), 麻 (Má), 寄 (Jì), 摆 (Bǎi) (and their Western internet cousins: “Cope,” “Seethe,” “Shill,” “Bootlicker,” “NPC,” “Lulz,” “Touch grass,” “Doomer”).

As the final chapter of our practical applications, we confront a survival question every modern citizen faces daily: in a digital ecosystem where recommendation algorithms and emotional contagion relentlessly herd human beings into binary diodes, how can we avoid being swallowed by the noise—neither becoming an angry megaphone for tribal rage, nor retreating into numb, sneering cynicism?

20.1 How Recommendation Algorithms Eradicate “Calibration (Shizhong)” and “Concrete Context”

Before blaming internet users for “having low literacy or failing to understand dialectics,” let us examine the digital ecosystem through a dialectical systems lens: the extreme polarization of contemporary online discourse is not happening because human brains suddenly mutated; it is happening because the underlying technical architecture of information distribution (engagement-maximizing recommendation algorithms and the attention economy) systematically punishes dialectical nuance and richly rewards one-sided extremism.

In Chapter 7 we studied Timely Equilibrium (Shizhong—calibration and concrete context), and in Chapter 4 we studied Xunzi’s “Laying Out All Things Side by Side (Jian Chen Wan Wu)”. Yet healthy dialectical thinking has three inherent physical traits: it requires paragraph length to state boundary conditions, it requires the reader to recruit slow deliberation (System 2) to weigh competing factors, and its conclusions are measured rather than delivering an instant dopamine hit of righteous outrage.

What, by contrast, is the objective function coded into modern short-video and social-media recommendation algorithms? Completion rate, dwell time, and immediate engagement velocity (likes, quote-retweets, and furious comment wars).

Trained on billions of user interactions, the algorithm quickly discovers an iron law of human neurobiology:

  • A rigorous 3,000-word analysis explaining that “Policy X yields net benefits under boundary condition A, but requires compensatory subsidies under boundary condition B” gets a 4% completion rate and a quiet comment section;
  • A 15-second clip set to pounding suspense music, headlined “SHOCKING! Group X Is Destroying Everything!” with all prior context edited out to leave only a 3-second flash of confrontation, triggers an adrenaline spike across a million viewers in three seconds and ignites 50,000 furious comments.

Consequently, twenty-four hours a day, the algorithmic filter performs two structural operations on human reality:

  1. Context Stripping: Taking a concrete event that occurred at a specific time, in a specific place, under a complex chain of prior causes (Chapter 7’s Time and Position), chopping off its head and tail, draining out all conditional context, and turning it into an abstract moral totem designed to trigger tribal reflexes;
  2. Echo-Chamber Welding: The moment you linger for five extra seconds on a one-sided take, the algorithm feeds you fifty consecutive posts with the exact same slant—coding Xunzi’s “blinded by a single corner and darkened to the overarching pattern” directly into your feed until you are viscerally convinced that “every sane person on earth agrees with me, and anyone who disagrees is either evil or brain-dead.”

Understanding how this algorithmic centrifuge works gives you your first layer of digital immunity: whenever a 15-second clip or viral screenshot on your phone makes your blood boil and makes your thumb itch to share or firing off an angry reply, freeze your thumb in midair for three seconds and ask yourself one question: “What happened in the five minutes BEFORE this clip started and the five minutes AFTER it was cut?”

Deep Dive Theoretical Mechanism: Cass Sunstein’s Law of Group Polarization and the Collapse of “Integrative Complexity”

Why is it that when like-minded people gather inside an algorithmically curated echo chamber, their views do not stay at their initial moderate level, but predictably race toward ever more extreme, black-and-white positions?

Harvard Law professor Cass R. Sunstein, in Going to Extremes: How Like Minds Unite and Divide (2009) and Republic.com, synthesized decades of social-psychology experiments to establish the underlying laws driving online polarization:

  1. The Law of Group Polarization: Homogeneous Deliberation Reliably Pushes Moderates Toward the Extreme
    • Intuitively, people assume that if ten individuals who mildly lean toward Position X (say, +2+2 on a scale from −5-5 to +5+5) sit in a room and discuss the topic for an hour, their post-discussion average will remain around +2+2.
    • Across hundreds of empirical trials worldwide, Sunstein and colleagues proved the opposite: whenever a deliberating group starts with a homogeneous slant (with no respected counter-voice present to offer Yan Ying’s “presenting the negative to perfect the positive”), after talking solely among themselves, the group’s average position jumps reliably toward +4+4 or +5+5!
    • Two mechanisms drive this shift: first, the Skewed Argument Pool—inside a homogeneous bubble, members hear dozens of new anecdotes supporting Side A and zero arguments for Side B, creating the statistical illusion of overwhelming certainty; second, Reputational Cascades (Social Comparison)—to signal tribal loyalty and moral purity inside an in-group, speakers instinctively stake out a position “half a step more radical than the previous speaker” (since nuance is policed as weakness or treason), igniting an ideological arms race toward the extreme.
  2. Peter Suedfeld and Philip Tetlock’s “Integrative Complexity (IC)” Collapse Model
    • Political psychologists Peter Suedfeld and Philip E. Tetlock developed a validated empirical scale to measure the dialectical sophistication of human speech and writing: Integrative Complexity (IC), scored from 11 to 77:
      • IC=1\text{IC} = 1 (Zero Differentiation, Binary Diode State): Recognizes only a single dimension and a single black-and-white rule, rejecting all exceptions or competing perspectives (“Anyone who supports X is 100% evil or stupid”);
      • IC=3–4\text{IC} = 3\text{–}4 (Differentiation): Recognizes that an issue involves multiple legitimate dimensions or competing stakeholder interests;
      • IC=6–7\text{IC} = 6\text{–}7 (High Dialectical Integration): Not only recognizes multiple competing dimensions, but explicitly maps their dynamic trade-offs, boundary conditions, and mutual synthesis.
    • Empirical studies by Suedfeld and Tetlock (Suedfeld & Tetlock, 1977; Tetlock, 1992) demonstrated a crucial neurocognitive law: when human beings are placed under conditions of information overload, time pressure (rapid-fire short-video scrolling), and high-arousal threat or moral outrage, their measured Integrative Complexity (IC) undergoes a sharp defensive drop—collapsing from multi-dimensional reasoning (4–54\text{–}5) straight down to single-dimension binary tribalism (IC=1\text{IC} = 1)!

This explains why defending calibration, context, and structural perspective in today’s online square requires swimming against a powerful current: you are pushing back against the combined force of algorithmic argument skew + tribal reputational cascades + high-arousal IC collapse.

★ 20.2 How to Stand Firm on a “Structural Perspective” in a Polarized Debate—Without Falling into Muddy Compromise

Confronted with a ferocious black-and-white shouting match online, where should a dialectical thinker stand?

Many people’s first instinct is: “Both camps are too extreme, so I’ll stand dead center at the 50% mark and say both sides have a point and both sides should be nicer.”—Never do that! As we proved in Section 14.1, handing out fifty lashes to each side without investigating causal structure or power asymmetry is Confucius’s xiangyuan (muddy compromise), and deserves every bit of the ridicule it gets as “spineless fence-sitting.”

Where a genuine dialectician stands in public debate is not at the flat arithmetic midpoint between Left and Right on the ground, but one dimension higher—on the Structural Perspective.

To make the difference between “muddy compromise” and a “structural perspective” unmistakable at a glance, let us compare four distinct modes of public commentary on the exact same viral news event:

[Event Background]: To crack down on traffic violations, a municipal government abruptly announces a strict campaign imposing heavy fines and a mandatory three-day account suspension on food-delivery riders caught speeding or riding the wrong way. On Day 1 of the crackdown, during a torrential rainstorm, several riders terrified of late-delivery penalties get into heated roadside altercations with traffic police, and clips of the confrontations go viral nationwide.

Within an hour, public commentary splits into four distinct modes:

  1. Mode 1: Binary Tribal Combatants (Single-Sided Blindness)
    • Extreme Pole A: “Delivery riders run red lights and ride on sidewalks all day long like road menaces! Fine them into oblivion and ban their accounts permanently!”
    • Extreme Pole B: “Hardworking gig workers risk their lives in pouring rain to earn pennies per order, and heartless traffic cops are bullying the poor!”
    • Diagnosis: Each camp grabs one real fragment of reality (pedestrian safety vs. gig-worker livelihood), demonizes the other human group on the street, and locks into an endless moral shouting match.
  2. Mode 2: Xiangyuan Muddy Compromiser (Section 14.1 Pseudo-Dialectic)
    • Script: “Everyone has it tough—riders work hard to make a living, and traffic police work hard to keep roads safe. Let’s all take a step back: riders should ride more slowly, and pedestrians and police should be more forgiving. Let’s fill the world with love!”
    • Diagnosis: Sounds warm and benevolent, yet is 100% useless—because the underlying physical mechanism forcing riders to speed in the rain hasn’t changed by a millimeter, and tomorrow’s rainstorm will produce the exact same collisions.
  3. Mode 3: Bureaucratic Boilerplate Reciter (Section 15.2 Pseudo-Dialectic)
    • Script: “Overall urban traffic governance remains stable. We must dialectically handle the relationship between road safety order and gig-economy employment—both defending the red line of traffic safety and safeguarding the legitimate rights of flexible workers, compacting primary responsibility across all parties.”
    • Diagnosis: Hollow ceremonial prose that leaves the unresourced “both… and…” double bind sitting right on the wet asphalt.
  4. Mode 4: Genuine Dialectical “Structural Analyst” (The Approach of This Handbook)
    • Analytical Trajectory:
      1. Look Past Surface Combatants to Map the Full Causal System (Chapter 11): The two parties colliding physically at the intersection are “riders” and “traffic police / pedestrians”—yet hiding invisibly behind the smartphone screen sits the player holding overwhelming governing dominance (the Principal Aspect of the Contradiction, Chapter 12): the delivery platform’s dispatch algorithm and penalty structure.
      2. Expose Unilateral Cost-Shifting Under Power Asymmetry (Section 14.5): The platform algorithm compresses promised delivery times to the physical limit where a rider cannot complete the route on time without speeding or cutting corners—allowing the platform to pocket 100% of the commercial premium of “30-minute ultra-fast delivery,” while externalizing 100% of the physical double bind (“obey traffic laws and get docked half a day’s pay for lateness, OR speed and risk police fines and crashes”) onto riders and pedestrians.
      3. Export Structural Mechanism Fixes (Chapters 6 & 7): Fining only individual riders treats the symptom while sparing the cause. A genuine structural solution brings the invisible Principal Aspect back into the legal accountability loop: municipal regulators audit the platform’s dispatch algorithm directly via API—whenever an order’s assigned delivery window is shorter than the physical travel time calculated at legal e-bike speed (15 km/h15\text{ km/h}) plus average red-light waits, the platform company is fined automatically for algorithmic inducement of traffic violations; and rainstorm/snow warnings automatically trigger a mandatory 15-minute weather extension switch (Jing and Quan).

Compare Mode 2 (Muddy Compromise) against Mode 4 (Structural Analysis): Mode 2 stands between two exhausted boxers in the ring pleading with them to smile at each other; Mode 4 looks up and asks: “Who built this ring and wrote the payout rules that force these two people to trade blows in the rain?” That is the real power of dialectical thinking in the public square.

Deep Dive Why Can a “Structural Perspective” Transcend Self-Interested Bias?—John Rawls’s “Veil of Ignorance” and Jürgen Habermas’s Communicative Rationality

In Section 20.2 we contrasted four modes of commentary on the delivery-rider crackdown. At this point, a realist reader might ask a sharp question: “In public controversies, isn’t everyone’s opinion determined simply by where they sit (‘where you stand depends on where you sit’)? Pedestrians pushing strollers naturally hate speeding e-bikes; hungry customers ordering lunch naturally want fast delivery; riders naturally hate fines; platform shareholders naturally want higher order density. Since everyone has their own self-interest, what gives Mode 4 (‘the Structural Perspective’) the right to claim it is more objective than the other three? How can a human being step outside their own self-interested seat?”

Two towering achievements of modern political philosophy—John Rawls’s “Veil of Ignorance” (A Theory of Justice, 1971) and Jürgen Habermas’s “Communicative Rationality” (The Theory of Communicative Action, 1981)—provide the exact operational tools for stepping outside single-seat bias:

  1. John Rawls’s “Veil of Ignorance” Role-Rotation Audit (Unblinding Self-Interest Bias)
    • Rawls proposed a famous thought experiment: if you want to test whether a public rule or institutional design is genuinely fair and structurally sound, imagine that all stakeholders must design the rule while standing behind a Veil of Ignorance—at the moment you vote on the rule, you do not know which role in this system you yourself will be assigned to once the curtain lifts tomorrow morning!
    • Strictly speaking, decision theory and political philosophy distinguish two ways of reasoning behind that curtain: Nobel laureate economist John Harsanyi modeled the veil as having an equal probability (say, 25% each) of landing in every role, maximizing expected average utility; John Rawls went even deeper—pointing out that behind a true Veil of Ignorance, you do not even know the probability distribution, and no rational person would gamble their basic survival floor on a coin toss! Therefore, rational rule-designers behind the veil apply the Maximin Rule (the Difference Principle): design the institutional structure so that even if the curtain lifts and I draw the shortest straw—landing in the single most vulnerable, disadvantaged role in the entire system—that role’s safety and survival baseline is maximized!
    • Apply Rawls’s Veil of Ignorance to our urban delivery system: once the curtain lifts tomorrow morning, you might wake up as the rider braving a thunderstorm to feed your family, the parent pushing a baby stroller across the crosswalk, the traffic officer on duty at the intersection, or the platform’s algorithm product manager.
    • When you evaluate the system from behind that veil (what Xunzi called “laying out all things side by side and suspending the balance-beam in the center”), notice what happens:
      • Both halves of Mode 1—“impose crushing fines on riders without touching the algorithm” (which ruins you if you wake up as the rider) and “let e-bikes speed through crosswalks unchecked” (which endangers your child if you wake up as the pedestrian)—are vetoed immediately behind the Veil of Ignorance because each breaches a vulnerable role’s survival floor!
      • The only institutional design that passes Rawls’s Maximin test is Mode 4’s Structural Co-Responsibility Architecture—requiring the dominant platform algorithm to surrender a fraction of its hyper-speed margin to guarantee physically safe delivery windows and rainstorm extensions, simultaneously protecting the pedestrian’s physical safety floor and the rider’s lawful livelihood floor.
  2. Jürgen Habermas’s “Strategic Action vs. Communicative Action”: Separating “Winning a Verbal Brawl” from “Solving a Shared Problem”
    • Habermas drew a fundamental distinction between two modes of human speech in the public sphere:
      • Strategic Action: My sole goal in speaking is treating the other person as an object to be manipulated, defeated, or silenced through rhetoric, tribal labels, or intimidation so my camp “wins” (the operating mode of flame wars and combat slang);
      • Communicative Action: My goal in speaking is working with other participants across three validity dimensions—objective factual truth, normative fairness, and subjective sincerity—to construct a structural solution that can withstand mutual scrutiny.

Thus, taking a Structural Perspective in public debate does not require pretending you are a disembodied angel with no personal interests; it simply requires pulling down Rawls’s Veil of Ignorance for sixty seconds—mapping the hard survival floors of every role in the system (and dragging the invisible dominant player back onto the diagram) before you speak.

★ 20.3 Character-by-Character Dissection and Countermeasures for the Ten Online Combat Slang Terms (“典、孝、急、蚌、乐、赢、润、麻、寄、摆”)

In today’s online comment sections and group chats, even when you write out a rigorous, well-sourced structural analysis like Mode 4 above, you will frequently run into a baffling reply: the commenter doesn’t address a single number in your post or point out a single flaw in your causal chain; instead, they toss a single Chinese character into the replies—“典” (Diǎn), “急” (Jí), “孝” (Xiào), “乐” (Lè), or “赢” (Yíng) (the exact functional equivalents of English internet replies like “NPC script,” “Cope and seethe,” “Corporate bootlicker,” “Lulz,” or “Tired of winning”).

What exactly are these ten ubiquitous single-character tokens—collectively dubbed the “Ten-Character Online Combat Mantra” (典、孝、急、蚌、乐、赢、润、麻、寄、摆)? Why are they so contagious that within a few years they have reduced countless public forums into single-syllable mud-slinging pits?

Before dissecting all ten characters one by one, let us examine the evolutionary game theory and pragmatic essence of this vocabulary through our dialectical lens:

In Chapter 15 we saw that Bureaucratic Dialectics evolved inside corporate and administrative hierarchies as a defensive linguistic shield—using wordy, hedged clauses (“generally controllable, both… and…”) to evaporate personal accountability and block falsification. Online combat slang evolved in the exact opposite habitat—anonymous, high-hostility, low-trust, hyper-fragmented comment feeds—as ultra-low-cost Anti-Dialogue Weaponry.

Look at the game-theoretic return-on-investment (ROI) inside an anonymous online argument:

  • If you play by the rules of rational discourse—spending 30 minutes checking primary sources, compiling statistics, and writing a 500-word structured argument (high cognitive investment)—a troll can spend 1 second replying with a single mocking character, derailing the thread at near-zero cost while your 500 words of concrete propositions expose multiple surface areas for nitpicking.
  • Conversely, if a commenter abandons reasoning altogether and fires off a single character devoid of subject, predicate, or factual assertion (such as “典” or “急”), their typing cost approaches zero (1 second), and because a single interjection contains zero falsifiable empirical propositions, you have no logical handle to refute! Better still, that single character executes an instant pragmatic hijack—shifting the spotlight from “Is your argument supported by evidence?” to “Look at this clown getting emotional / reciting a script!”, handing the drive-by commenter an instant hit of cheap superiority as they stroll away.

Thus, Bureaucratic Boilerplate and Online Single-Character Slang are two faces of the exact same anti-rational coin: bureaucratic prose uses verbose hedging to make accountability evaporate, while combat slang uses one-syllable labels to induce brain-death in dialogue.

Understanding why these tokens evolved does not mean surrendering to them. On the contrary, once we strip open the pragmatic sleight of hand, psychological defense mechanism, and logical fallacy inside each of the ten characters, we can spot them instantly, disarm them with a single surgical sentence, and immunize our own minds against linguistic decay.

Below, we group the ten characters into six functional categories and conduct a full forensic dissection, live scenario reconstruction, and counter-script design for each:

Category I. “典” (Diǎn — “So Classic” / “Classic NPC Script”): Abusing Pattern Recognition to Bypass Substantive Refutation

  • Etymology & Surface Meaning: Short for Jīngdiǎn (经典, “classic / textbook specimen”). When you post an analysis and someone replies with a lone “典” (or “Peak classic,” “Time to open the anthology,” akin to English “Classic NPC dialogue tree” or “Bingo card complete”), the literal message is: “I’ve seen this exact talking point eight hundred times online; you’re just parroting a stock script with zero original thought.”
  • Pragmatic Attack Mechanism & Logical Fallacy: Why does a single syllable “典” leave earnest writers momentarily tongue-tied? Because it executes a covert Dismissal by Categorization (what C. S. Lewis called Bulverism). In human cognition, pattern recognition (filing new stimuli into familiar mental folders) is a normal energy-saving heuristic. The user of “典”, however, deliberately conflates two completely independent logical questions: “How frequently has an argument been stated before (Frequency)?” versus “Are the empirical facts and causal logic of the argument actually true (Truth Value)?” Suppose someone asks why a dropped stone falls toward the earth, and you explain Newton’s law of universal gravitation; the other person sneers: “So classic (典)! Trotting out that tired old gravity script again!” Does the fact that universal gravitation—or the law of supply and demand, or basic thermodynamics—has been stated ten thousand times before make it false? On the contrary, foundational truths recur frequently in debates precisely because they describe objective physical or economic constraints that cannot be wished away! The cunning of “典” is that it never names which sentence in your post is factually wrong, nor does it state what its alleged “template” actually says; it simply slaps a “stale rerun” sticker across your entire argument and tosses it in the bin, absolving the commenter of the intellectual duty to answer your evidence.
  • Underlying Psychological Defense: Intellectual laziness disguised as world-weary superiority. When confronted with a detailed argument that contradicts their prior bias—and which they lack the domain knowledge to refute line by line—admitting they can’t refute it hurts their ego; tossing out a “典” lets them role-play a jaded master who “has seen through every trick in the book.”
  • Live Scenario & Dialectical Counter-Script:
    • Scenario: In a debate over an electric-vehicle highway crash test, an automotive structural engineer posts: “Judging A-pillar deformation requires checking the overlap percentage (25% small-overlap vs. 100% full-width frontal) and the squared relative impact velocity, not just a single still photo.” A commenter immediately replies: “典! The PR damage-control script arrives right on cue—peak classic!”
    • Counter-Operation (Polite Ledger Audit: Invite Them to Recite the “Template” and Pinpoint the Error): Never reply with “You’re the classic one!” Instead, adopt Chapter 4’s calm Socratic posture and politely ask them to name which line of physics is wrong: > Counter-Script: “Could you specify which exact template my mention of the ‘25% small-overlap crash standard’ and the ‘kinetic energy v2v^2 formula’ copied from? If high-school physics formulas count as ‘classic (典)’ because engineers use them every day, then sure, physics is pretty classic. Better yet, could you point out which of the three sentences above contains a factual or physical error? If you can’t point to a single factual error, replying with a single syllable ‘典’ just to dodge checking the crash-test parameters might be the most overused template on the internet.”
    • Inward Self-Check: Before hitting send, take five seconds to audit yourself too—did I actually ground my comment in the specific primary data of this case, or did I reflexively paste a generic talking point without reading the details? If the latter, let their “典” serve as a free reminder to sharpen your own specificity.

Category II. “急” (Jí — “You’re Triggered / Salty / Mad”): Swapping Emotional Attribution for Factual Truth

  • Etymology & Surface Meaning: Born in competitive gaming and forum flame wars, whenever Speaker A writes a detailed multi-paragraph reply, uses a serious tone, or responds more than once, Speaker B drops “急了” (Jí le — “He’s triggered / He’s mad / Hit a nerve!”) or “破防了” (Pòfáng le — “Defense broken / Salty”) (the exact equivalent of English “U mad bro?” or “Cope and seethe”). The subtext is: “Look how many words you typed—obviously I hit your sore spot and made you lose your cool, which proves I won and you lost.”
  • Pragmatic Attack Mechanism & Logical Fallacy: In formal and informal logic, this is a hyper-compressed hybrid of an Ad Hominem Fallacy and a Red Herring. Its entire trick consists of a single move: stealthily swapping the scoreboard of the conversation. Originally, the scoreboard on the table was “Is objective proposition PP supported by evidence and logic?” The moment the troll realizes they are losing badly on the factual scoreboard of proposition PP and cannot answer your data, they kick over the factual scoreboard and unilaterally declare that today’s contest is scored on “Who typed fewer words and looks more aloof and unbothered (the Emotional Posture Scoreboard)”! Notice how this sets up a textbook Section 14.5 Double Bind:
    • If you write 300 words citing data to refute them →\rightarrow “Look how much you typed—you’re triggered (急了)!”
    • If you reply in one crisp, forceful sentence →\rightarrow “Look how aggressive your tone is—you’re triggered!”
    • If you ignore them and stop replying →\rightarrow “Look, he ran away speechless because his defense broke—definitely triggered!” Logically, a speaker’s physiological arousal level (whether they are calm as a cucumber or indignant at a dangerous falsehood) has zero causal bearing on the truth value of the empirical data they present! If an innocent person falsely accused of theft slams their alibi receipt onto the courtroom table in anger, does the judge convict them because “Look, he slammed the table—he got mad”?
  • Underlying Psychological Defense: Psychological projection under helplessness. The person typing “急了” is almost always the one who has run out of factual ammunition; by preemptively slapping the “emotional / triggered” sticker onto your forehead, they project their own inadequacy onto you and fantasize that they are a puppet-master pulling your strings.
  • Live Scenario & Dialectical Counter-Script:
    • Scenario: A medical researcher writes an 800-word post citing three peer-reviewed clinical trials to debunk an influencer’s dangerous claim that “an extreme raw-meat ketogenic diet cures diabetes and cancer.” A fan of the influencer replies in the comments: “Whoa, writing a whole essay—did exposing your medical racket make you mad (急了)?”
    • Counter-Operation (Rigid Decoupling of Mood from Data): Never try to prove “I’m not mad, I’m totally calm!” (The instant you defend your mood, you validate their swapped scoreboard.) Instead, expose the scoreboard swap openly and pin the ball right back onto the empirical numbers: > Counter-Script: “Whether I typed this while smiling over iced coffee or frowning at my keyboard does not change a single digit in the three clinical trial tables above showing LDL-cholesterol and renal-load spikes. In a factual discussion, trying to guess the other person’s mood to declare victory is the standard exit move when someone has zero counter-data in hand. If you have peer-reviewed clinical data showing the opposite, post the DOI link—we audit numbers here, not moods.”

Category III. “孝” (Xiào — “Filial Bootlicker / Shill”) and “润” (Rùn — “Love It or Leave It / Just Run”): Motive Assassination and Revoking Standing to Speak

Why do we pair “孝” (Xiào) and “润” (Rùn)—two slang terms usually shouted by opposite ideological camps—inside the same analytical section? Because through a dialectical structural lens, these two apparent enemies are 100% isomorphic twins built on the exact same logical fallacy!

  • Etymology & Surface Meaning:
    • “孝” (Xiào — literally “filial piety,” weaponized as “Corporate/Brand/Foreign Bootlicker,” e.g., “Apple filial son,” “Xiaomi filial son,” “Big-Tech bootlicker”): The instant you acknowledge a single concrete technical strength of a company, brand, institution, or country (or correct a fabricated rumor about it), someone slaps the “filial son (孝子)” label on you—implying “You worship that corporation/entity like your biological father; you’re a spineless shill.”
    • “润” (Rùn — phonetic loan from English “Run,” meaning to emigrate; weaponized in arguments as “If you don’t like it here, why don’t you just run/leave?!”): The instant you point out a concrete defect in a local policy, domestic product, industry practice, or neighborhood, someone barks back: “If you think it’s flawed, then 润 (get out)! Why are you still complaining here?!”
  • Pragmatic Attack Mechanism & Logical Fallacy: Look at the shared logical equation powering both “孝” and “润”: both execute an Appeal to Motive (Argumentum ad hominem circumstantial) combined with Revoking the Speaker’s Standing, and both rest on an absurd 100% Bundled Binary Axiom (All-Good or All-Evil):
    • The hidden false axiom behind “孝”: Entity XX is 100% pure evil; therefore, anyone who acknowledges even a single positive metric about XX must be motivated by slavish emotional devotion or paid shilling. It completely erases the everyday dialectical reality that a rational consumer can criticize Apple’s signal reception on Monday and praise Apple’s M-series chip energy efficiency on Tuesday based on lab benchmarks.
    • The hidden false axiom behind “润”: For any system YY in which you live or work, you must either praise 100% of its corners unconditionally, OR if you dare point out a single loose screw, you are a traitor who hates the entire system YY and must physically leave it (“love it or leave it”). This directly violates Yan Ying’s principle in Chapter 5 (“the minister offers corrections where there is a flaw so as to bring the good to completion”) and Chapter 10’s Sublation—it is precisely because I live inside this house and want the roof not to leak on my family that I point out which roof tile is cracked! The person who sees a leaking roof tile and screams “Anyone who mentions the leak should get out and sleep under a bridge!” is the one guaranteeing the roof collapses on everyone’s head!
  • Underlying Psychological Defense: Totem worship and binary splitting defense. In the minds of those who shout “孝” and “润”, the world consists not of falsifiable concrete problems, but of sacred vs. demonic tribal totems. Unable to tolerate the cognitive dissonance that “something I dislike has a technical strength worth learning from” or “something I love has a real flaw that needs fixing,” they assassinate the speaker’s loyalty to kill the messenger.
  • Live Scenario & Dialectical Counter-Script:
    • Scenario: A hardware reviewer posts a benchmark video showing that “Phone Brand X’s new periscope telephoto algorithm achieved the highest low-light resolution in our lab test” (prompting bullet comments screaming “Paid filial son [孝子]!”), and two minutes later points out that “Brand X’s OS hides ad-opt-out toggles four menus deep, which badly hurts user experience and needs fixing” (prompting another wave of comments screaming “If you hate domestic brands so much, just 润 and buy foreign phones!”).
    • Counter-Operation (Unbundle “Total Loyalty Trials” Back into “Component-Level Fact Checks”): > Counter-Script Against “孝” (Bootlicker): “Acknowledging that a product leads on Metric A neither means the product is flawless nor means adopting the manufacturer as a relative. If recognizing a competitor’s real technical strength is taboo, then covering our eyes and pretending rivals have zero strengths is just lying to ourselves. Let’s stick to whether the telephoto sensor’s measured MTF resolution chart is accurate.” > Counter-Script Against “润” (Love It or Leave It): “Pointing out which tile on our own roof is leaking is how we get the tile patched so everyone inside stays dry; if we tell everyone who spots a leak to move out, until only people who chant ‘the roof is 100% waterproof!’ remain inside, the rain will soak everyone’s bed. Fixing problems takes patching leaks, not banishing the people who point them out.”

Category IV. “乐” (Lè — “Pure Lulz / Spectator Cynicism”) and “蚌” (Bèng — “Can’t Hold Back My Laugh”): Dissolving Serious Reasoning with Nihilistic Sneers

  • Etymology & Surface Meaning:
    • “乐” (Lè — “Hilarious,” “Pure comedy,” “Lulz-seeker / 乐子人”): Confronted with a serious policy debate, a structural analysis, or another person’s genuine predicament, the commenter contributes zero constructive reasoning and drops a lone “乐”—declaring themselves an aloof, untouchable spectator in the grandstand who views all human effort and suffering as a circus act for their amusement.
    • “蚌” (Bèng — homophonic shorthand for Bēng bù zhù le [绷不住了], “Couldn’t keep a straight face / Burst out laughing”): Means “Your argument is so laughable that my facial muscles gave out.”
  • Pragmatic Attack Mechanism & Logical Fallacy: In informal logic, this is the Appeal to Ridicule (the Horse Laugh Fallacy). Derisive laughter is one of humanity’s oldest status-dominance displays. When someone replies to your structured argument with “乐” or “蚌”, they execute a zero-cost shortcut around Chapter 3’s Reductio ad Absurdum: to prove a premise absurd legitimately, Zeno or Mozi had to walk step by step through a rigorous deductive chain; the user of “乐 / 蚌” skips 100% of the deductive steps and jumps straight to the theatrical sound effect of laughing in your face! By laughing first, they broadcast a social cue to bystanders: “This person’s post is so beneath contempt that it doesn’t even warrant a counterargument—anyone who takes it seriously is a sucker.” At the same time, the “Lulz-seeker (乐子人)” posture serves as invincible ego armor: because I care about nothing, stand for nothing, and propose nothing, I can never lose or be disappointed—I sit safely in the bleachers jeering at everyone down in the arena who dares to sweat and build.
  • Underlying Psychological Defense: Defensive cynicism born of prior disillusionment. Many young people who armour themselves in “乐” and “蚌” once cared deeply and spoke earnestly, only to feel burned by clickbait manipulation or helplessness; to protect themselves from ever feeling foolish again, they put on the mask of universal mockery—“If I laugh at everything serious first, nothing serious can ever hurt me.”
  • Live Scenario & Dialectical Counter-Script:
    • Scenario: A labor-law practitioner writes a detailed, step-by-step guide on how employees in a specific industry can document unpaid overtime and file for statutory arbitration. The top comment consists of two characters: “乐, 蚌 (Lol, can’t hold back my laugh).”
    • Counter-Operation (The Spotlight Question: Force the “Dry Laugh” to Explain the Joke in Daylight): Nothing deflates a performative sneer faster than calmly turning on the house lights and asking the person to explain specifically which line was funny: > Counter-Script: “Could you share whether the part that made you laugh was Article 41 of the Labor Law cited in Paragraph 2, or the timestamped evidence-preservation checklist in Paragraph 3? If there is a practical legal loophole in any of those steps, pointing it out concretely will help everyone avoid a pitfall; if you can’t name a single flawed step, then meeting a practical guide for protecting workers’ rights with an empty sneer doesn’t save anyone a single minute of unpaid overtime.”

Category V. “赢” (Yíng — “Winning So Hard I’m Numb / Tired of Winning”): Using Totalizing Irony to Erase Calibrated Local Progress

  • Etymology & Surface Meaning: “赢” (Yíng) or “赢麻了” (Yíng má le — “Winning until numb” / “Double win means I win twice”) originally emerged as a healthy grassroots satire against Section 14.8 (Mechanical Thesis–Antithesis–Synthesis that reports only good news) and Section 14.9 (Pollyanna propaganda that spins every disaster as a triumph). When sensationalist media outlets proclaimed “We won again!” regardless of what happened, netizens coined “Winning until numb (赢麻了)” to puncture the boast.
  • Pragmatic Attack Mechanism & Logical Fallacy (How an Antidote Mutates into a Poison): Recall Chapter 8’s law of reversal at the extreme: while “赢麻了” started out as a cognitive-unblinding pin to pop inflated propaganda balloons, once it became an unthinking knee-jerk reflex, it mutated into the exact opposite extreme—Totalizing Irony and Cynical Nihilism! In the eyes of someone addicted to spamming “赢”, the world no longer has any quantitative calibration (Chapter 9). Whether you are discussing a verified engineering breakthrough (say, a domestic medical stent or MRI component cutting patient costs from $15,000 to $2,000) or a concrete municipal reform that measurably reduced administrative wait times, the instant a factual report contains any positive number, they don’t even glance at the data before sneering: “Yep, yep, winning until numb (赢麻了)! Another massive win!” Logically, this commits a Universal Negative Fallacy that is the exact mirror image of blind boasting: “Because I once saw someone exaggerate a boast in the past, from today onward every real local improvement and engineering advance on earth is dismissed sight unseen as fake news.”
  • Underlying Psychological Defense: Hegel’s “Unhappy Consciousness” (unglückliches Bewusstsein) and learned suspicion—preemptively sneering at all good news to prove to oneself that “I am too smart to be fooled, unlike the gullible masses.”
  • Live Scenario & Dialectical Counter-Script:
    • Scenario: A public-health researcher posts a provincial dataset showing: “After three high-incidence rare-disease targeted therapies were added to the reimbursement formulary this year, average annual out-of-pocket costs for patient families dropped from 180,000 RMB to 24,000 RMB; however, county-level hospital pharmacy stocking rates currently sit at only 35%, leaving a critical last-mile bottleneck.” A commenter immediately replies: “Don’t ask—if you ask, we’re winning until numb (赢麻了)! Just be grateful and move on!”
    • Counter-Operation (Anchor on Calibrated Baselines; Reject Both Unconditional Cheerleading and Totalizing Sneers): > Counter-Script: “Nobody here is chanting ‘total victory’ slogans. For those several thousand rare-disease families, out-of-pocket cost dropping from 180,000 RMB to 24,000 RMB is life-saving real money (Verified Fact A); at the same time, 65% of county hospital pharmacies still lacking stock is an urgent bottleneck that must be fixed (Verified Fact B). Whether someone closes their eyes and shouts ‘everything is perfect!’ or closes their eyes and sneers ‘winning until numb!’ to erase a 156,000-RMB real cost reduction, both are binary diode habits that refuse to read actual numbers.”

Category VI. “麻 / 寄 / 摆” (Má / Jì / Bǎi — “Numb / GG Doomed / Let It Rot”): Defensive Forfeiture and Exiting the Table

  • Etymology & Surface Meaning:
    • “麻” (Má — “Numb / Burned out / Just destroy it all, I’m tired”): Declaring that one’s emotional and cognitive receptors have blown a fuse under chronic information overload or pressure;
    • “寄” (Jì — phonetic Chinese slang for gaming’s “GG” [Good Game], meaning “Totally doomed / It’s over / Dead on arrival”): Declaring the entire game 100% lost the moment a single headwind or setback appears;
    • “摆” (Bǎi — short for Bǎilàn [摆烂], “Let it rot / Quiet quitting / Deliberately tanking the game”): “Since effort can’t guarantee an easy win, I’ll proactively throw my controller on the floor and stop trying altogether.”
  • Pragmatic Attack Mechanism & Logical Fallacy: Whereas 典, 急, 孝, 润, 乐, and 赢 are offensive weapons aimed at silencing an opponent, 麻, 寄, and 摆 are white flags of total capitulation aimed at shutting down problem-solving itself. Logically, they take a Chapter 8 “cyclical downturn” or a Chapter 11 “local bottleneck” and extrapolate it in 0.1 seconds into 100% Irreversible Cosmic Doom (Universal Fatalism)—if the whole world is already “GG doomed (寄了),” then four-form diagnostics, reframing, and seizing the principal contradiction are all pointless, and the only smart move is lying flat alongside them.
  • Underlying Psychological Defense: Martin Seligman’s “Learned Helplessness” and “Self-Handicapping” ego protection. Psychologist Martin Seligman demonstrated that after an organism experiences repeated shocks it cannot control, it stops trying to escape even after the cage door is opened wide. Even more subtly, in social psychology, proactively shouting “It’s doomed, I’m letting it rot (寄了，我摆烂了)” functions as Self-Handicapping (Berglas & Jones, 1978): “If I try my hardest, study the method, and still fail, that proves I lack ability (which bruises my ego); but if I announce upfront that I’m not even trying (‘I’m letting it rot!’), then when I fail, my ego stays safe because I can tell myself I could have won if I’d actually tried!”
  • Live Scenario & Dialectical Counter-Script:
    • Scenario: A study group or project team is discussing how to restructure their portfolio or pivot customer acquisition during an industry downturn, and a member spams three messages in a row: “Stop trying—the macro environment is 100% GG doomed (寄了), the whole industry is numb (麻了), just let it rot (摆烂) and wait to die.”
    • Counter-Operation (Unbundle the “Uncontrollable Macro Cycle” from the “Controllable Micro Margin,” and Know When Not to Argue):
      • Layer 1 (Toward Anonymous Strangers in Public Comment Feeds): If a stranger replies to your analysis with a lone “Numb (麻了)” or “Doomed (寄了),” remember: recognition is the finish line—do not argue back. They have publicly announced they are unplugging their keyboard and leaving the table; you can never wake someone who has climbed into a sack to sleep.
      • Layer 2 (Toward Teammates, Friends, or the Voice Inside Your Own Head): Apply Chapter 7’s Level Unbundling and Chapter 12’s Sun Tzu Principle (“Invincibility lies in oneself” ) to slice open the totalizing word “Doomed”: > Counter-Script: “Industry growth slowing from 30% to 5% means macro tailwinds have cooled—it does not mean 100% of practitioners drop to zero (breaking universal fatalism). Even in a harsh contraction where 50% of players wash out, the remaining 50% who upgrade their craft absorb the exited market share. We cannot control tomorrow’s macro GDP (vulnerability lies in the environment), but we control 100% whether the portfolio we send out at 5:00 p.m. today is ten times sharper than the average applicant’s (invincibility lies in oneself). Using ‘macro headwinds’ as an excuse not to polish our work this afternoon only guarantees that three months from now we actually end up in the ditch.”

  3. Ruthlessly Filter Your Interlocutors (Save Your Sharpest Steel for Worthy Minds): Chapter 4’s Socratic dialogue and Steelmanning require real cognitive energy. Only if the other person responds to your first prompt by dropping the slang and putting concrete reasons or data on the table have they earned a second reply from you; if their second reply is another single-character sneer or personal insult, close the tab immediately—protecting your finite attention for people and problems that matter is the highest respect you can pay your own life.

Common Pitfall: Don’t Mistake Harmless Self-Deprecating Humor Among Friends for Malicious Combat Slang

When applying Section 20.3, remember Chapter 7’s calibration (Shizhong).

When a close friend vents in a private chat after a grueling week of overtime, “I’m totally numb / doomed this week (麻了/寄了),” or when an author finishes writing a thorough 500-word evidence-backed analysis and caps it off with a light self-deprecating joke—that is normal emotional decompression and human humor.

There is only one criterion separating harmless humor from toxic anti-dialogue slang: is the word a pinch of seasoning alongside a substantive argument, or is it a gag used to replace argument and stuff the other person’s mouth shut?

Case Analysis: Detoxifying and Reconstructing a Viral Public Controversy

Recently, a well-known bakery chain announced a strict corporate rule: every night after closing at 9:30 p.m., store clerks must take all unsold fresh bread baked that day, cut every loaf into pieces with scissors on camera, and throw them into the trash bin—strictly forbidding employees from taking unsold bread home or handing it out free to unhoused people outside, on pain of immediate termination. When a video of clerks cutting up fresh bread went viral, the comment section exploded into two warring camps:

  • Top Upvoted Comment A: “While the rich feast, food rots! They’d rather shred fresh bread into garbage than let poor people eat a bite—capitalists have pitch-black hearts! Peak classic (典中典)!”
  • Top Upvoted Comment B: “Did the bleeding-heart saints upstairs get triggered (急了)? The bakery paid for the flour, they can throw it away however they want! If they give it away free and someone gets a stomachache and sues the store for 50,000 RMB, are you going to pay the settlement for the owner? Lulz (乐)!”

Using this chapter’s Structural Perspective alongside the tools of Chapters 5–7, step above the binary shouting match and slang of Comments A and B to write a 300-word structural analysis: first, identify which real constraint Comment A and Comment B each saw (and what each missed); second, design a battle-tested Third Alternative (Reframing the Option Set) that prevents good food from being destroyed while protecting the bakery from moral hazard and legal liability.

Reference Analytical Approach (Designed to demonstrate how to cut through online flame wars and engineer an institutional solution):

  1. Step 1: Strip Away the Combat Slang and Reconstruct Both Sides’ Real Structural Constraints
    • Comment A sees the genuine ethical pain point of food waste alongside unmet human need. Comment B, beneath its obnoxious “triggered / lulz” slang, points to two real commercial and legal deadlocks that any retail bakery operator faces:
      1. Moral Hazard & Price-System Cannibalization (Chapter 8 Reverse Transformation): If clerks are allowed to take unsold bread home for free at 9:30 p.m., store staff have a perverse incentive to over-bake at 7:00 p.m. or hide popular loaves in the back room until closing; if the bakery hands out free bread at the door every night at 9:30 p.m., customers who normally buy at full price at 8:00 p.m. will simply wait until 9:30 p.m. for free handouts, collapsing full-price evening revenue.
      2. Food-Safety Liability Risk: If a free cream-filled pastry is taken home, left unrefrigerated for two days, and causes food poisoning, under strict food-safety liability statutes the bakery faces heavy fines and lawsuits.
    • Thus, the store manager “shredding bread” is not acting out of cartoon villainy, but executing a crude defensive stop-loss in the absence of a better institutional mechanism.
  2. Step 2: Reframe the Alternatives (Chapter 6)—Use Institutional Design to Remove the Obstacle Blocking “Zero Waste” and “Commercial Safety”
    • Since the two structural obstacles are “free handouts cannibalize full-price sales and invite internal gaming” and “unregulated giveaway carries liability risk,” retail bakeries across Europe, North America, and Asia have already validated a mature Surplus Food Blind-Box & Food-Bank Custody Mechanism (such as Too Good To Go / Leftover Magic Bags):
      1. Price & Blind-Box Insulation (Protecting Full-Price Sales): Instead of $0 handouts or letting shoppers cherry-pick specific items, after 9:00 p.m. the bakery releases a limited number of “Late-Night Surprise Blind Bags” via a mini-program (e.g., 3 random unsold pastries originally worth 45 RMB sold as a sealed blind bag for 15.9 RMB—covering raw ingredient and utility costs, while the random assortment and uncertain availability prevent full-price daytime shoppers from waiting around).
      2. Good-Samaritan Third-Party Food Bank Custody: For any items remaining at 9:30 p.m., individual clerks are never permitted to pocket inventory; instead, certified municipal Food Banks / Community Charities sign a standardized Surplus Food Cold-Chain Transfer & Liability Waiver Agreement (backed by Good Samaritan food-donation statutes) to pick up and distribute items directly to inspected senior centers and shelters. Replacing moral screaming with institutional engineering is how genuine dialectical thinking solves public deadlocks.
